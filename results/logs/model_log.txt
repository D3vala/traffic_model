Specification C is used. Comparing the three is not a formality — they
disagree, and the disagreement is itself the finding.

B assigns reporting a large elasticity and moves EDSA's decline sharply
toward zero. That correction is inflated: B's covariate is total feed
volume, which CONTAINS the crash counts being modelled, so the outcome
partly explains itself. An earlier version of this study used B and
concluded that most of EDSA's decline was a reporting effect. That
conclusion was resting on a circularity.

C removes the circularity and the reporting elasticity falls to a small
value. EDSA's decline barely moves. The reason is visible in the
correlation printed above: the reporting proxy and the time trend both
fall across the window, so they compete for the same variance, and the
trend term absorbs most of it. The regression cannot settle how much of
the decline is reporting and how much is incidence.

What survives all three specifications is the CONTRAST. EDSA falls and
C5 rises in every one of them, and C5's increase is never smaller than
+28% per year. That is the robust result. The size of the reporting
correction is not robust, and this study does not claim it.

Reading the coefficients:
  EDSA baseline rate     : 2.8649 per 100,000 passages
  C5 vs EDSA             : x0.380
  EDSA monthly trend     : x0.9834 per month (-18.2% per year)
  C5 monthly trend       : x1.0219 per month (+29.7% per year)
  reporting intensity    : elasticity 0.15 (1.0 would mean counts track posting volume one for one)

The two trends have opposite signs, and that contrast is the finding.
C5 rises while EDSA falls across the same 18 months, on the same feed,
with exposure already divided out and reporting intensity controlled.

The two are not equally secure, and the write-up should not present them
as though they were. C5's increase is robust: it holds under every
reporting specification, and it occurred while the feed was getting
quieter, which means a reporting explanation would have to argue that
MMDA posted less overall while posting more about C5 specifically.

EDSA's decline is NOT secure. A placebo series of non-crash reports —
stalled vehicles, roadworks, things with no bearing on safety — falls at
a similar rate, which is what a reporting effect looks like. But the
regression cannot confirm it, because the reporting proxy and the time
trend are correlated and compete for the same variance. The honest
position is that this study cannot separate the two, and therefore
cannot report EDSA's decline as a safety improvement. It is not that a
reporting artefact has been proven; it is that one cannot be ruled out.

Robustness — the EDSA:C5 count ratio:
  falls from 3.87 to 1.84 across the window (-35.0% per year)

  Numerator and denominator come from the same feed on the same days,
  so any reporting change affecting BOTH corridors in the same
  proportion cancels exactly. The decline is therefore not explained
  by the account simply posting less overall.

  It does NOT rule out a DIFFERENTIAL reporting change. If MMDA shifted
  attention toward C5 over these months — a new camera, a redeployed
  patrol, an operational focus — the ratio would fall with no change in
  either corridor's actual safety. Nothing in this dataset distinguishes
  that from genuine convergence, and the write-up must say so rather
  than claiming the stronger result.

  EDSA: mean signed error -25.5 — the model under-predicts the held-out months
  C5: mean signed error -38.2 — the model under-predicts the held-out months

  The errors are not random noise: C5 is under-predicted in every
  held-out month and badly so in the last two. The model was fitted
  through October 2019 and the C5 increase accelerated after that, so
  the fitted trend is too shallow for what followed. This is a real
  signal rather than a defect in the fit, and it strengthens rather
  than weakens the C5 finding — but it also means the frozen-trend
  simulation baseline is conservative for C5.

  Read this as a sanity check, not as a performance claim. Four held-out
  months per corridor is a small test set, and the model is predicting
  within the same 18-month regime it was fitted in. It establishes that
  the model is not merely memorising its training months; it does not
  establish that it would forecast 2025.
